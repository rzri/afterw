I have two appliances I trust more than most software: a gaiwan and a terminal. One steeps leaves, the other steps through a folder of text, and neither one has ever tried to hold my attention hostage.

Plenty has been written about both, so I'll keep it short and personal — why they belong together.

## The gaiwan

Three pieces of ceramic. Leaf in, water in, pour out. Nothing to charge, nothing to configure, no dark pattern to unlock "Pro". You cannot be interrupted *into* it the way an app interrupts you: the water decides the pace, and you can only watch and wait.

## The terminal

A cursor, a path, `rg` and `git`. The output is text, always. Nothing recomputes it behind your back, no loading spinners, no "you might also like". It does exactly what you asked, a little toon fast, and then sits still again.

## The click of things slowing down

The interesting part is the mood they share. Both reward stillness: a good cup is the one you didn't rush to finish; a good note is the one you let sit overnight. Put the two together — tea steeping while `git diff` scrolls — and you get a small, quiet machine for thinking, with no ping in sight.

That's the whole pitch. Slow in, slow out, text underneath.
