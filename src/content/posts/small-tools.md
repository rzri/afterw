Not everything needs to be a product. Most of what I build is a tool small enough
to explain in one sentence, big enough to save a recurring annoyance. A running
list, kept updated:

- **`tidy`** — a script that renames screenshots by their capture date and dumps
  them into a folder, so my desktop finally stays empty.
- **`quell`** — a tiny RSS filter that hides anything from sites I've flagged as
  noise, before it reaches my eyes.
- **`jot`** — a keyboard-first scratchpad that saves every note as a dated `.md`
  file, because the "quick notes" app keeps changing its storage model.

## Why tiny wins

A small tool has a small contract. Fix it in an afternoon, explain it in a
paragraph, delete it without grief when the problem dissolves. Somewhere between
"too small to matter" and "big enough to maintain" sits a pleasant little island,
and I intend to keep building huts there.
