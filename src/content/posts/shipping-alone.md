I spent a year shipping small things by myself. Not a startup, not a portfolio —
just a cadence of tiny, finished projects. Here are the notes I wish I'd had on
day one.

## Finished beats clever

The projects that grew did nothing fancy. They had a single clear promise, a
screenshot that explained them, and a one-line readme. The clever ones
accumulated abstractions and occasionally an audience of exactly one (myself).

## The boring launch

People overestimate the big reveal. I underrated the small hand-off: posting the
thing where its users already sit, linking the demo above the repo, and writing
one sentence about *why*. Distribution is mostly just showing up where the
problem lives.

## The honest part

Solo shipping is quiet. No one owes you feedback, and momentum is a single
diff. I learned to treat the finished, unglamorous release as the win and every
round of feedback as a gift rather than an entitlement. It changed the pace of
everything after.
