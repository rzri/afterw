description: "A short walk at the edge of the village, when the light finally goes soft."

There is a ten-minute walk I take most evenings, out past the last house and along the edge of the rice fields. I don't go for the exercise. I go because, at dusk, the fields decide to be look like themselves.

The light goes soft — not grey, not warm, but some in-between that photographers would kill for and painters can't name. The treeline turns into a strip of almost-black, and the sky above it holds its colour a beat too long, the way a held note hangs.

## The quiet

What I notice is how little there is to *do*. No notifications find me out here. The only pings are crickets. It is the rarest kind of landscape: one that asks nothing of you. It's enough to be in it.

## The same walk

Every evening it's the same field and the same path, and every evening it's not. The mist is different, the level of the water in the canals is different, the one heron is either there or it isn't. Repetition, it turns out, is not the enemy of noticing. It's the invitation.

I come back with the porch light on and supper waiting, carrying a little of that grey-gold dusk in with me.

Some places you don't need to travel far to find. You just have to be there at the right hour.
